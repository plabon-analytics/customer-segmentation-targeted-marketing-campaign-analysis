Campaign performance before segmentation

Six campaigns. All producing single-digit acceptance rates — sent to every customer regardless of behavior.

The most recent campaign nearly doubled the historical average — reaching ~14.8% without any formal segmentation. This raises the key question this project answers: if an accidental marginal improvement produced this result, what could a deliberate, data-driven segmentation strategy consistently achieve?

What the data revealed before modeling

Six findings from EDA that directly shaped the segmentation strategy.

0.78
Catalog → Total Spend correlation
The strongest relationship in the entire dataset. Catalog buyers are the most reliable predictor of high total spend — confirmed before the model ran a single iteration.
0.72
Past campaigns → future response
Customers who engaged with historical campaigns are strongly likely to respond to future ones. Campaign engagement is consistent and predictable — not random.
0.67
Income → spend (imperfect)
Income and spend are correlated — but not cleanly. Wide scatter at every income level proves demographics alone cannot identify valuable customers.
−0.20
Recency → campaign response
Customers who have not purchased recently also tend not to respond to campaigns. Behavioral disengagement happens on both dimensions simultaneously.
10×
Spend gap within one customer base
From $108 to $1,179 average spend — a 10× difference across segments all receiving the same campaign message at the same cost per contact.
73%
Budget reaching zero-converters
1,640 of 2,205 customers have never responded once. Roughly 73% of campaign budget was reaching customers with a confirmed zero-conversion history.

Champions and High Value Disengaged are demographically near-identical — similar age, income, and tenure. Yet one group responds to campaigns at 35.3% and the other at 0.0%. Demographic targeting would have grouped them together and served both the same offer. Behavioral segmentation is what separated them — revealing the single most important strategic distinction in the entire dataset. This one finding is the analytical justification for the entire project.

Method, briefly

Business problem defined before a single line of code was written.

Steps 1–2
Define, then explore
Business problem and 6 analytical questions set first. EDA across demographics, spend, channels, campaigns, correlations, and outlier detection followed.
Steps 3–4
Clean & engineer
Every cleaning decision traced to an EDA finding. Six new features built — Total Spend, Total Purchases, Age, Tenure, Campaign Score, Has Children.
Steps 5–6
Score & cluster
Quartile-based RFM scoring across 5 dimensions — chosen over equal-interval scaling due to right-skewed distributions. K-Means applied to the scored features.
Steps 7–8
Profile & recommend
Four segments named and validated against real campaign response rates. Every recommendation traced back to a specific data point from the profiles.
K=4
not K=2

The silhouette score technically peaked at K=2 (0.3739) — but two segments are too broad to act on. K=4 (0.2764) was chosen because it produces four independently actionable customer groups, and the Elbow Method confirmed a clear bend at K=4–5. Analytics exists to serve business decisions, not mathematical perfection.

What this actually means

The complete project in plain language — no jargon, no code.

"Imagine running a shop where four completely different types of customers walk through the door every day. The first type — your Champions — spend heavily, come back regularly, and raise their hand every time you announce something new. The second type — your High Value Disengaged — also spend heavily and return regularly, but they completely ignore every flyer, email, and campaign you send. They buy entirely on their own terms. The third type — your Budget Conscious customers — come in often and genuinely want to buy more, but they're managing tight household budgets with children at home. And the fourth type — your Dormants — used to come in, but you haven't seen them properly in a while.

Now imagine you've been printing one flyer and handing it to all four groups identically — same message, same offer, same timing. Your Champions already buy so the flyer is redundant. Your High Value customers throw it away every time. Your Budget Conscious customers would love a relevant deal but the offer doesn't match their budget. And your Dormants have already mentally moved on.

That is exactly what this data showed was happening. And that is exactly what four targeted strategies fix — without spending an extra rupee."
— Plabon Roy, Customer Segmentation & Targeted Marketing Campaign Analysis
